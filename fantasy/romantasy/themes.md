Common 'Romantasy' Themes

There is a misconception out there that Romantasy is just regular fantasy with a lot of kissing. The critics will tell you it's shallow. They will say it lacks the depth of "serious" literature or the complexity of grimdark fantasy.

But they are wrong.

When you actually look at the bestsellers in this genre, you realize they are rarely just about the romance. The romance is the engine, sure. But the car is usually built on much heavier stuff.

We are talking about life and death stakes. We are talking about the loss of self. We are talking about the brutal reality of war.

If you want your Romantasy to resonate, you can't just have two pretty people staring at each other. You need to anchor their relationship in themes that matter.

Here are the specific themes that show up time and time again in the biggest hits of the genre.

The Transformation from Survivor to Sovereign

In almost every single Romantasy bestseller I analyzed, the protagonist starts in the exact same place.

The bottom.

They are usually starving. Or they are hiding a secret power that could get them killed. Or they are oppressed by a tyrannical government or a cruel family. Their primary goal in the first act is never "to save the world."

It is simply to survive the day.

But that creates a problem.

If your character stays in survival mode for the whole book, you don't have a story. You have a tragedy.

So the central theme becomes the shift from merely surviving to actually living. It is about the transition from being a pawn in someone else's game to becoming a player on the board.

And this transformation usually comes with a heavy price.

The protagonist has to shed their old identity. They have to stop hiding that secret magic or that royal lineage they didn't know they had. They have to accept that they are powerful, even if that power terrifies them.

In many of these stories, the love interest acts as the catalyst for this change. The love interest usually sees the protagonist's true potential long before the protagonist does.

But ultimately, the theme is about agency.

It is about a character realizing that keeping their head down and following the rules isn't going to save them. It is about realizing that if they want to protect the people they love, they have to become something else entirely. Something dangerous.

They have to stop being the victim and start being the queen, the warrior, or the monster.

The Cost of Sacrifice and the Greater Good

You want to know the quickest way to kill the tension in a book?

Let your protagonist have everything they want without giving up anything.

Romantasy thrives on the idea that love and duty are often at war with one another. And this leads to the second major theme I see everywhere in this genre.

The terrible cost of doing the right thing.

We see this constantly with the "One vs. The Many" dilemma. The protagonist is almost always forced into a situation where they have to choose between saving the love of their life or saving the kingdom.

Or sometimes it's even darker.

Sometimes they have to choose between their own humanity and winning the war.

We see protagonists who have to become vampires to defeat vampires. We see characters who have to break the heart of the person they love to keep a vital secret. We see heroes who are willing to let the world burn just to keep one person safe.

This is why the "Enemies to Lovers" trope works so well here. It inherently carries the theme of sacrifice.

If you love the enemy, you are sacrificing your loyalty to your own side. If you kill the enemy, you are sacrificing your heart.

There is no easy answer.

And that is the point.

The theme compels the reader because it asks a question we all struggle with. How much of ourselves are we willing to lose for the things we believe in?

In these books, victory is never free. The magic always demands a price. The throne always demands blood.

If you want your story to land, your protagonist needs to pay that price.
